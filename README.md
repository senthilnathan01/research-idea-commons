# Research Idea Commons

A platform that discovers, cites, organizes, and shares public research, project, and startup ideas so more people can work on meaningful problems.

## Why

The internet is full of valuable ideas that never become projects. Researchers, lab members, indie hackers, founders, and technical writers often publish questions or unfinished ideas in blogs, GitHub repos, newsletters, X posts, LinkedIn posts, or personal notes.

This pattern shows up wherever public discussion moves faster than individual execution capacity. People working near the edge of a field often generate more questions, project directions, and half-formed possibilities than they can personally pursue. Some of those ideas may be genuine gems, but they are scattered, hard to find, and easy to forget.

At the same time, many students, builders, researchers, and early founders say they lack good project, research, or startup ideas. There may be a mismatch: some people have more ideas than time, while others have time, energy, and skill but need better ideas to work on.

## Idea

Build one central platform that actively finds public ideas from across the internet, cites the original source, organizes them clearly, and helps people discover ideas they can execute.

This should not be a private advantage or a hoarded knowledge base. The goal is to create a net-positive system where good ideas are easier to find, original thinkers get credit, and more people can collaborate to make useful things happen.

## What It Does

- Finds public research questions, project ideas, and startup ideas from blogs, GitHub, newsletters, X, LinkedIn, lab pages, and other public sources.
- Preserves attribution by citing the original author, source, and link for every idea.
- Converts scattered posts into structured idea entries.
- Organizes ideas by domain, difficulty, freshness, required skills, execution path, and potential impact.
- Places ideas, problem statements, and partial solutions into a meaningful semantic space so nearby concepts can be discovered.
- Connects the dots between related ideas: one post may describe the problem, another may contain part of the solution, and a third may fill a missing piece.
- Helps builders find ideas they can actually work on.
- Helps researchers and idea authors get visibility and credit.
- Allows people to track whether an idea is unclaimed, being explored, funded, implemented, or completed.
- Creates a future path for collaboration, team formation, and funding around promising ideas.

## Core Principles

- Attribution first: every idea must point back to the original source.
- Public-source only unless explicit permission is given.
- Do not steal ideas; make them discoverable, credited, and easier to execute.
- Help match idea-rich, time-constrained people with execution-ready people.
- Support open collaboration over private hoarding.
- Reveal hidden connections between related problems, partial solutions, and missing pieces.
- Focus on net-positive outcomes: more useful projects, research, products, and startups should exist because this platform exists.

## Idea Format

Each idea should have a consistent structure:

- Title
- Original author or source
- Original link
- Domain
- Short summary
- Why it matters
- Possible execution path
- Required skills
- Current status
- Related problems
- Related partial solutions
- Missing pieces
- Related links or prior work

## MVP Questions

- Which sources should be supported first: GitHub, blogs, newsletters, X, LinkedIn, or curated manual submissions?
- How can the platform detect whether a post contains a real idea, a question, or just general commentary?
- How can ideas and problem statements be embedded so semantically similar items appear near each other?
- How can the platform identify when one idea has a problem statement, another has a partial solution, and combining them creates a stronger path forward?
- How should the system show these suggested connections without pretending they are proven solutions?
- What is the minimum citation needed to fairly credit the original thinker?
- How should authors request removal, correction, or ownership changes?
- How should the platform avoid misrepresenting someone's unfinished thought as a fully endorsed proposal?
- Should users be able to claim an idea, fork it, join a team, or propose a collaboration?
- How should funding work in the future without turning the platform into a low-quality idea marketplace?
- How should duplicated or similar ideas be merged while preserving all original sources?
- What signals should rank ideas: novelty, feasibility, impact, credibility, demand, or community interest?

## First Version

Start with a curated, public, read-only idea directory. Manually collect high-quality ideas from public blogs, GitHub repos, newsletters, and social posts. Each entry should include a clear summary, citation, original link, tags, and status.

The first version does not need complex automation. It only needs to prove that scattered ideas can be collected, credited, organized, and made useful for people who want to build, research, or start something meaningful.
